# Terrebonne bike path — evidence and legal-authority index

**Prepared:** 17 November 2026. **Status:** preliminary, public-source research for possible Québec municipal-law consultation. **Not** a legal opinion, court filing or independent certification of the linked documents.

## Most consequential findings

1. **CM16 1452** — The original Montréal city council minutes from the December 19–20, 2016 sitting contain resolution CM16 1452 (item 30.02, file 1160615005, based on CE16 1973). This is a standing acceptance for certain future borough offers to undertake roadwork on the City's arterial network under Charter article 85. The resolution does **not** expressly say “modification mineure,” nor specifically approve removing protected cycling lanes. Whether it still applies to the Terrebonne proposal is unresolved. [Original minutes, PDF p. 75](https://ville.montreal.qc.ca/documents/Adi_Public/CM/CM_PV_ORDI_2016-12-19_13h00_FR.pdf#page=75).
2. **CM18 1378** — Another advance acceptance, in the November 19, 2018 minutes, was explicitly for **2019–2021**. A post-2021 renewal or other delegation would need to be identified before invoking it for 2026. [Original minutes, PDF p. 58](https://ville.montreal.qc.ca/documents/Adi_Public/CM/CM_PV_ORDI_2018-11-19_13h00_FR.pdf#page=58).
3. **Borough dossier 1266880005 (November 2, 2026)** — Its own timetable says: **“Novembre 2026 : Le CM devra accepter l'offre du CA (via une résolution).”** This documents the administrative timetable's expectation, not a binding legal conclusion. The same file plans for a December borough signage resolution and changes on the ground. [Decision file, PDF p. 5](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/2026-11-02-borough-council/raw/CA_Cdn_dossier_1266880005_excerpt_2026-11-02.pdf#page=5).
4. **Comparable 2024 authorization** — The original project was described as transitional signage/marking work, yet the borough offered the service by CA24 170018, the city separately accepted the 2024 offers including Terrebonne via CM24 0179, and the borough later adopted traffic ordinance CA24 170131 / OCA24 17042. This is relevant precedent but does not by itself establish the requirements for the 2026 proposal. [2024 city decision file](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/2024-project-approval/raw/CM_dossier_1248935001_excerpt_2024-02-19.pdf).

The November 2 decision file specifically states that removal of the protected with-traffic lane in winter would put those cyclists in mixed motor-vehicle traffic; the protected contraflow lane would remain. It also says operational and safety feasibility still needs technical confirmation, including emergency-service issues. The project is described as reversible/temporary, with no civil road reconstruction. - [Off-street parking historical/aerial study](https://openmobilitydata.github.io/Terrebonne-open-sources/1954-underwriters-plans-terrebonne-garages/): estimates that 72% of checked buildings either had a historical garage or visible present-day driveway/pad. **Not** a count of presently usable spaces or a household-level capacity survey.
- [Winter cycling data, access request 2026-300](https://github.com/OpenMobilityData/Terrebonne-access-requests/tree/main/2026-300): evaluate counter periods, directional caveats and revisions before citing counts as evidence.

## E. Legal consultation / potential urgency

Potential questions for Québec municipal-law counsel: Does an extant blanket acceptance cover this change? Is cycling-network jurisdiction separate from arterial roadwork? What resolution/ordinance is being challenged? What standing would an individual resident have, and what evidence of imminent harm would be needed for interim relief?

**Do not treat this index as a draft injunction.** A court application would require a specific actionable decision or imminent work, admissible evidence, correct parties and procedure. The Montréal civic rights charter's commitments are relevant policy context but are not by themselves a court cause of action (see Charter art. 86.1).
